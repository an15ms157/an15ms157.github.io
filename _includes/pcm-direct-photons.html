An informal introduction to my work

At the LHC, ALICE studies collisions between lead nuclei travelling at nearly the speed of light. The energy deposited in their overlap region can create a quark–gluon plasma (QGP), in which quarks and gluons are no longer confined inside individual hadrons. This resembles the matter that filled the universe microseconds after the Big Bang. In central collisions, the fireball’s evolution through the plasma and hadronic stages lasts roughly 10–13 fm/c (about 3–4 × 10−23 s), up to the last hadronic interactions.[1]

Interactions in the expanding matter can reshape the signals carried by hadrons. Photons do not participate in the strong interaction and usually escape with little further scattering, preserving information about the conditions in which they were produced.[2]

To measure photons from the collision, we use the Photon Conversion Method (PCM). It utilizes the interactions between photons and detector material that produce electron–positron pairs. We identify these pairs and reconstruct their tracks to estimate the original photon’s momentum.[4] Only a fraction of photons convert and are reconstructed, so measuring the photon yield also requires corrections for conversion probability, detector acceptance, reconstruction efficiency, and background. Knowing the detector’s material distribution accurately is essential.[3,7]

So what?

Photons offer clues to the fireball’s heat. After accounting for photons from particle decays and prompt production, the extra photons at low momentum are consistent with radiation from the hot matter.[2] The plot suggests a temperature scale of around 3.5 trillion kelvin. Estimating the initial temperature requires modelling how the fireball expands and cools.[7,8]

Looking further ahead, ALICE 3 is proposed for LHC Run 5 and beyond. Its programme includes precise measurements of thermal electron–positron pairs to constrain how the QGP temperature evolves. These pairs are emitted by the hot matter itself, unlike the conversions in detector material used by PCM.[5]
